A Review of Modern Art

the board game

Chilean edition

Modern art is a full fledged board gaming classic. First released in 1992, this auction game by Reiner Knizia is still considered one of the best ever created.

The basic premise of Modern Art is that each player is a museum, attempting to sell the most valuable paintings at the end of each round. A painting becomes more valuable as more paintings by the same artist are auctioned.

Importantly, players are also the auctioneers, and thus they can pick and choose which artists become more valuable.


Players do this by playing cards, and are thus limited by what artists are in their hand.

What makes this a classic to me is the relatively simplicity of the concept, and yet the meaningful impacts of each rule. Each card has an auction type, and some auction types will favor certain players (for example, a set-price auction would favor whoever is next in turn order as they have the first chance to buy it).

And, players are increasily invested in the artists they have bought into as the round goes on, causing some asymmetry in player goals.


My favorite part of about the game however is the vast number of different printings featuring a wide variety of artists. It's like how Monopoly gets stickered with every brand imaginable, but if it was nice looking art instead.
